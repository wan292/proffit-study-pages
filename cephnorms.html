Cephalometric landmarks 16

SSella — Midpoint of the cavity of sella turcica.
NNasion — Junction of the frontal & nasal bones (frontonasal suture), anterior.
PoPorion — Top of the external auditory canal (anatomic) / ear-rod (machine).
OrOrbitale — Lowest point on the inferior margin of the orbit.
BaBasion — Lowest point on the anterior margin of the foramen magnum.
CoCondylion — Most posterosuperior point on the mandibular condyle.
BoBolton point — Highest point in the retrocondylar fossa of the occipital bone.
PtmPterygomaxillary fissure — Base of the fissure where anterior & posterior walls meet.
ANSAnterior nasal spine — Tip of the ANS (Harvold: point where it is 3 mm thick).
PNSPosterior nasal spine — Most distal point of the horizontal process of the palatine bone.
APoint A (subspinale) — Innermost contour of the premaxilla, between ANS and the incisor.
BPoint B (supramentale) — Innermost contour of the mandible, between the incisor and chin.
PogPogonion — Most anterior point on the bony chin.
GnGnathion — Most anterior-inferior point on the chin.
MeMenton — Most inferior point of the symphysis (bottom of the chin).
GoGonion — Midpoint of the ramus-body contour (the mandibular angle).

Reference planes 7

★ S–N (anterior cranial base)Sella to nasion. The most common cranial reference; lies ~6–7° above Frankfort anteriorly.
Frankfort Horizontal (FH)Porion → orbitale. The 1882 Frankfort-conference reference; both landmarks are hard to locate.
Palatal planeANS → PNS. Maxillary reference for vertical/rotational change.
Occlusal planeFunctional (molar–premolar, used by Wits) vs molar–incisor (Downs).
★ Mandibular planeGo → Me (or Go → Gn). Basis of FMA / SN-MP vertical assessment.
Nasion perpendicularVertical line from N, perpendicular to FH (McNamara): maxilla on/just ahead, mandible just behind.
★ Natural Head Position (NHP)True horizontal recorded with the patient relaxed — preferred over anatomic planes in modern cephalometrics.

The first widely-used analysis — relates jaws & teeth to S-N.

Tweed triangle ★Charles Tweed

Diagnostic triangle for positioning the lower incisor.

FMA (FH–MnPl)25°
FMIA (L1–FH)65°
IMPA (L1–MnPl)90°
Sum= 180°
FMA range22–28°

Watch: High FMA → keep IMPA low; the triangle drives extraction/incisor decisions. 6

Wits ★Alexander Jacobson

AO–BO measured on the functional occlusal plane — a check on ANB.

Males (AO–BO)−1 mm
Females0 mm
Cl IImore positive
Cl IIInegative

Watch: Independent of nasion, but only as reliable as the occlusal plane it is drawn to; says nothing about which jaw. 6

McNamara ★James McNamara, 1984

Combines Ricketts + Harvold; jaws to the nasion perpendicular, lengths from condylion.

Maxillaon / slightly ahead of N-perp
Mandibleslightly behind N-perp
Midface (Co–A) & mand (Co–Gn)grow together
LAFHANS–Menton
NormsMichigan / Burlington / Bolton

Watch: Norms not taken in NHP, so it uses anatomic Frankfort rather than true vertical. 6

Ricketts Robert Ricketts

Lower incisor & lips referenced to the A–Pog and E-lines.

L1 to A–Pog+1 to +2 mm
L1 inclination≈22°
E-line (lips)at / 1–2 mm anterior
Facial axis≈90°

Watch: A-Pog moves with treatment, so the incisor target is a moving reference. 6

Sassouni Viken Sassouni, 1955

First to relate vertical AND horizontal proportions; arcial.

Anterior arcthrough N, ANS, Pog
Posterior arcthrough S, Go
Well-proportionedhorizontal planes converge to one point
Coined“skeletal open bite”

Watch: Full arcial analysis is rarely used today; the convergence concept survives. 6

Harvold Egil Harvold

Unit lengths from condylion; tooth-position-independent.

Maxillary unitCo → ANS
Mandibular unitCo → Gn
+ Lower face heightquantified
Harvold differencemand − max length

Watch: Linear only — needs age/sex tables (Burlington); no AP jaw-to-cranium reading. 6

Jarabak (Björk) Björk / Jarabak

Posterior : anterior face-height ratio = growth-direction read-out.

S–Go : N–Me ratio62–65%
< 62%hyperdivergent / open-bite
> 65%hypodivergent / deep-bite
Sum of polygon angles≈ 396°

Watch: A proportion, not a position — pair with an AP analysis. 6
