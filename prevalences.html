The Big Picture — who has malocclusion?

  • Only about one-third of the US population has normal occlusion; two-thirds have some malocclusion. Ch5 Fig 5.1
  • Of those with malocclusion, not more than 5% have a specific known cause — the rest are complex inherited + environmental. Ch5 Fig 5.1
  • Angle classes (US children/youth): Class I / normal 30+%, Class II ~15–20+%, Class III <~5%. Ch1 p8
"1/3, 2/3, 5%" — a third are fine, two-thirds aren't, and only a tiny slice have a cause we can name.

Crowding & incisor alignment

  • Age 8–11: just over half of US children have well-aligned incisors. Ch1 p6
  • Moderate (usually crowded) irregularity: 20–25%; severe/extreme: ~15%. Ch1 Fig 1.9, p6
  • Lower arch is more crowded than upper at every severity, and worsens into adulthood. Ch1 p6
"Half aligned, 15% bad" — half the kids are fine; ~1 in 7 is severe.

Anteroposterior — Class II / III

  • Normal overjet = 2 mm. Overjet ≥5 mm (≈ Class II): 22% children → 13% youth → 9% adults. Ch1 Fig 1.12, p7
  • Only ~one-third have ideal AP incisor relationship; another third only mildly increased. Ch1 Fig 1.12
  • International: Class II ~25% of Danish children; Class III 3–5% Japan, ~2% China (+ 2–3% pseudo-Class III). Ch1 p8
  • Class II → more common in White / Black; Class III → more common in Mexican-American / Black. Ch1 Fig 1.11
Increased overjet (Class II) is much more prevalent than reverse overjet (Class III).

Vertical — deep bite / open bite

  • Normal overbite = 2 mm. About half the population has ideal vertical. Ch1 Fig 1.13
  • Deep bite is much more prevalent than open bite. Deep bites lessen with age; open bites stay stable. Ch1 p7
  • Severe deep bite ~2× as common in Whites; open bite >3 mm more common in Blacks. Ch1 Fig 1.11
  • Significant deep bites affect ~20% of patients. Ch11
"Deep beats open" — and deep bite is a White trait, open bite a Black trait.

Transverse (crossbite) & diastema

  • Posterior crossbite: 7–8% of US population — 7.1% in children 8–11, 8% youth, 9% adults. Ch1 p6 · Ch11
  • Crossbite differs little between racial/ethnic groups. Ch1 Fig 1.11
  • Midline diastema >2 mm: 26% of US children; persists in >6% of youth/adults. Ch1 p6
  • Blacks are >2× as likely to have a diastema as Whites/Mexican-Americans. Ch1 p6
  • Spontaneous closure most likely if diastema is <2 mm (closes as canines erupt). Ch1 Fig 1.5
Crossbite ≈ 7% (kids) and diastema = 26% (kids) — both from the same NHANES kid group.

Dental anomalies — extra / missing / impacted

  • Supernumerary teeth: 90% are in the anterior maxilla (mesiodens = most common). Ch11 p~290
  • Maxillary canine ectopic eruption / impaction: 1–2% range. Ch11 p~410
  • Most-often-missing (evolutionary): 3rd molars, then maxillary lateral incisors & mandibular 2nd premolars. Ch1 Fig 1.16
  • Serial mixed-dentition problems (ectopic/impacted/missing) affect <4% of the mixed-dentition population. Ch11
  • Early loss of one primary canine → midline shift only 1.3 mm vs 1.0 mm normal (not clinically significant). Ch11 (Christensen)
"90% supernumeraries up front, 1–2% canines stuck" — both maxillary anterior problems.

Cleft & craniofacial

  • Orofacial clefts = the most common congenital defect of the head/neck. Ch5
  • Cleft palate: 1 in 700 births; cleft lip & palate (CLP): 1 in 1600 (US). Ch5
  • Clefts: 30% syndromic, 70% unknown origin. Ch5
  • Craniosynostosis: 1 / 2200 births, 25% syndromic. Ch5
  • Pierre Robin Sequence: 60% are syndromic (Stickler most common). Ch5
"700 / 1600 · 30% syndromic" — and Robin flips it: 60% syndromic.

Trauma & TMD

  • Permanent incisor trauma: 15–18% of youth aged 12–18. Ch1 p10
  • ~1 in 3 chance of injury to a protruding incisor (overjet >5 mm + incompetent lips). Ch1 Fig 1.18
  • Previous trauma + age <9 → risk of re-injury is 8.4× higher → retract incisors. Ch1 p10
  • TMD present in 5–30% (depends on symptoms counted); moderate malocclusion in 50–75%. Ch1 p10
  • Malocclusion–TMD correlation is weak: only 0.3–0.4 → orthodontics is rarely the primary TMD treatment. Ch1 p10
"1-in-3 protruding incisor, 8.4× if re-hit young." TMD link is weak (0.3–0.4).

Habits

  • Habit appliances (e.g. crib) extinguish thumb-sucking in 85–90% of cases. Ch11

⚠ Confusable traps — don't swap these

  • Class II by age 22 / 13 / 9 (falls) vs crossbite 7 / 8 / 9 (rises) — opposite directions with age.
  • Diastema 26% (children, >2 mm) vs persists >6% (adults) — don't quote 26% for adults.
  • Cleft palate 1/700 vs CLP 1/1600 vs craniosynostosis 1/2200 — three different denominators.
  • Clefts 30% syndromic but Pierre Robin 60% syndromic — Robin is the high one.
  • Supernumerary 90% anterior maxilla vs canine impaction 1–2% — don't merge the figures.
  • Normal occlusion ~⅓ (35%) vs known-cause ≤5% — the 5% is of the malocclusion group, not the whole population.
  • Normal overjet 2 mm = normal overbite 2 mm — same number, different plane.
