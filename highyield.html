Ch 1 Orthodontic Need, IOTN & Study Design 3 HY · 9 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
IOTN — two components
Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need = DHC (Dental Health Component) + AC (Aesthetic Component); used in the UK; there is only average correlation between the two components.
IOTN-DHC — grades
5 grades. Grade 1 = no need, Grade 2 = little need, Grade 3 = moderate/borderline need, Grade 4 = severe need (definite treatment need), Grade 5 = extreme/very great need.
IOTN grade for impinging/complete overbite with soft-tissue trauma
Grade 4f — increased and complete overbite WITH gingival or palatal trauma = severe need (4f). (The 'f' subcategory = overbite with trauma.)
IOTN grade for impacted canine + retained primary canine + supernumerary case
Grade 5 — impaction (impacted teeth) and supernumeraries push the case to grade 5 (extreme need); impacted canines are the most important feature, not the supernumerary.
IOTN — what it does and does NOT consider
DHC ranks by the single worst occlusal feature; it does NOT use cephalometric readings. It measures/ranks treatment need and difficulty; craniofacial anomalies are handled separately.
Key of occlusion (Angle)
The MAXILLARY (upper) first molar — Angle's 'key to occlusion' is the upper first permanent molar; classification is read from the mesiobuccal cusp of the upper first molar relative to the lower first molar buccal groove.
Case-control study
A study comparing a group WITH the disease/outcome (e.g. cancer patients) to a group WITHOUT it, looking backward for exposures = case-control study (retrospective, observational).
Most important finding in the impacted-canine case
Impacted canines (high/unerupted canines with retained primary canines while premolars have erupted) are the key feature — not supernumeraries.
IOTN for a 90-degree rotated central from a supernumerary + impacted canines + crowding
Grade 5 — severe rotation, impaction and supernumerary-driven displacement give the highest (extreme need) grade.

Ch 2 Concepts of Growth and Development 9 HY · 19 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
Cephalocaudal gradient of growth
An axis of INCREASED growth extending from the head toward the feet (head grows/matures first, then proceeds caudally toward the feet).
Scammon's growth curve — order/first to grow
NEURAL tissue grows first and completes earliest. Four curves = Neural, General (somatic), Genital, Lymphoid. By age ~6 the NEURAL curve is essentially complete; Lymphoid peaks/overshoots in late childhood then declines; Genital grows mainly at puberty.
Growth chart percentile threshold for referral
A patient/child plotting ABOVE the 95th–97th percentile should be REFERRED for ENDOCRINE consultation (not a bone scan).
Best theory of mandibular (jaw) growth
FUNCTIONAL MATRIX theory (Moss) — bones grow secondarily in response to functional/soft-tissue demands; growth determined by EXPANSION OF THE ORAL AND NASAL CAVITIES (not by nasal cartilage, cranial base, or adjacent bone/cartilage).
Mechanism of mandibular growth
Direct DEPOSITION posteriorly (posterior border of ramus + condyle) with RESORPTION/REMODELING anteriorly (ramus remodels forward); the whole mandible is DISPLACED forward/downward by cranial-base growth moving the TMJ. It is NOT the same as maxillary growth.
Type of ossification — cranial vault
INTRAMEMBRANOUS ossification (direct), with sutures present from birth allowing expansion as the brain grows.
Type of growth — cranial base (synchondroses, e.g. spheno-occipital plate)
ENDOCHONDRAL ossification / INTERSTITIAL growth at the synchondroses (cartilaginous growth) — distinct from the membranous vault.
Hydrocephaly effect on cranial vault
Cranial vault INCREASES in size — the membranous vault enlarges passively in response to the expanding brain/CSF (functional matrix in action).
Craniosynostosis
PREMATURE FUSION of cranial sutures = craniosynostosis (the named condition when sutures fuse early, e.g. in a 9-month-old).
Measurement/experimental approaches for studying growth
MEASUREMENT approaches = ANTHROPOMETRY and CEPHALOMETRICS. EXPERIMENTAL/marking approaches = Vital staining (alizarin/madder), Tetracycline labeling, and Metallic (radiopaque) implants — distinguish 'measurement' from 'experimental' methods.
Best diagnostic for condylar hyperplasia/hyperactivity
Nuclear bone scan with GAMMA-EMITTING ISOTOPE TECHNETIUM-99m (Tc-99m) — detects active condylar growth (NOT alizarin/vital staining, which is experimental).
Lip thickness — age of maximum
Lip thickness reaches its MAXIMUM during ADOLESCENCE (then decreases thereafter).
Mandible growth relative to Meckel's cartilage
The mandible grows LATERAL and POSTERIOR to Meckel's cartilage (mandible forms by intramembranous ossification lateral/posterior to the cartilage, which largely disappears).
Remnant of the perichondrium of Meckel's cartilage (first pharyngeal/branchial arch)
The SPHENOMANDIBULAR LIGAMENT (along with the malleus and incus) — derived from the perichondrium of Meckel's cartilage of the 1st arch. (Stylomandibular ligament is from the 2nd arch.)
Modeling/growth of the palate
RESORPTION on the NASAL floor (superior surface) + DEPOSITION on the ORAL/hard-palate side (inferior surface) — the palate moves downward, enlarging the nasal cavity.
Golden standard to assess growth STATUS/potential
Cervical Vertebral Maturation (CVM) is the chosen answer for assessing current growth status/whether growth has stopped; growth POTENTIAL/long-term is studied via longitudinal serial records (anthropometric + cephalometric).
Most common cause of childhood facial asymmetry
CONDYLAR FRACTURE / condylar injury (trauma) — most common cause of facial asymmetry in a child (when occlusion is otherwise unremarkable).
Cause of TMJ ankylosis with posterior open bite in a child
SEVERE INFECTION (or trauma) of the CONDYLE damaging the condylar growth site — leads to unilateral posterior open bite, pain, and progressive mandibular ankylosis.
Functional matrix — what it is NOT determined by
NOT determined by nasal cartilage (that is the nasal-septum/cartilage theory of Scott), NOT by cranial base growth, NOT by intrinsic growth of adjacent bone/cartilage — it IS determined by EXPANSION OF THE ORAL AND NASAL CAVITIES (functional spaces).

Ch 3 Early Stages of Development (Growth & Dental Development) 7 HY · 24 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
Infantile (infant) swallow — defining feature
Active contraction of the LIP (perioral) musculature with the tongue tip brought forward into contact with the LOWER lip. NOT tongue palatal to upper incisors, NOT posterior-tongue activity.
Infant swallow — duration / disappearance
Normal infantile swallow pattern disappears within the first ~1.5–2 years of life (as posterior teeth erupt).
Onset of mature (adult) swallow
Adult/mature swallow pattern is established by about age 12 years.
Adult (mature) chewing pattern — trigger
Achieved when the PERMANENT CANINES erupt (not permanent molars, not primary canines).
Primate (anthropoid) space — maxilla
Located MESIAL to the canine, i.e. between the lateral incisor and the canine.
Primate (anthropoid) space — mandible
Located DISTAL to the canine, i.e. between the canine and the first primary molar.
Flush terminal plane + forward (favorable) mandibular growth
Ends up as a Class I molar relationship.
Mesial step terminal plane + forward mandibular growth
Ends up as Class I (a mesial step is already favorable for Class I).
Distal step terminal plane + growth
Ends up as Class II (a distal step does NOT correct to Class I; may be Class II end-to-end).
Rate/mechanism of tooth eruption
The apex/apical area stays in essentially the same place while the CROWN moves occlusally (eruption ≠ apex moving).
Calcification of primary canine — onset
Begins in utero at approximately 17 weeks of gestation.
Neonatal line in primary teeth
Forms at birth; appears in the enamel/dentin laid down from birth to ~3rd month (accentuated incremental line marking birth).
Hand-wrist radiograph — bone that signals the growth/pubertal spurt
The SESAMOID bone (of the thumb/MCP joint) — its ossification indicates approach of the growth spurt. (Distractors: hamate, radius.)
CVM stage CS3 (cervical vertebral maturation) — interpretation
Peak mandibular growth is still ~1 year AHEAD ('growth peak is in a year or so'), making it ideal timing to begin growth modification.
CVM stage CS5/late stage (Fig 3.12 picture E) — interpretation
Patient is MORE than 1 year beyond the peak of growth (spurt has passed).
Definition of growth modification (explaining to a parent)
Producing MORE mandibular growth than would occur normally — NOT merely 'accelerating/speeding up' growth that would happen anyway.
Best study type to predict/assist an individual child's growth
LONGITUDINAL studies (same individuals followed over time) — superior to cross-sectional for individual growth prediction.
Secular trend of growth — definition
Successive generations show MORE RAPID growth and EARLIER maturation (children grow faster and mature earlier than prior generations).
Class III ceph: SNA 78, SNB 74, ANB −2 in a growing child with reverse overjet
Describes a true GROWING (skeletal) Class III patient (negative ANB), not merely a functional shift / pseudo Class III.
Radiograph showing multiple unerupted/supernumerary teeth + retained deciduous (Fig 3.15)
Classic for CLEIDOCRANIAL DYSPLASIA (multiple supernumeraries + retained primary teeth + failure of eruption).
Impacted maxillary canine — immobile primary canine, no labial bulge, root of 'c' resorbed, permanent canine very close
Best treatment = EXTRACT the primary canine (interceptive extraction to allow eruption/self-correction); traction reserved for true impaction.
Leeway space / arch length when primary 2nd molars are still present (9 y/o, mild crowding)
Arch length will DECREASE as primary molars are replaced by smaller premolars (mesial drift of molars uses up the leeway space).
Premature loss of primary first AND second molars (moderate crowding)
Results in REDUCED arch length and reduced arch circumference (space loss from molar mesial migration).
Dental age determination from panoramic radiograph
Assessed by stage of tooth calcification/eruption (e.g., reported dental ages of ~8 and ~9–12 in the recall stems) — graded by developing dentition, not chronological age.

Defined by active lip-musculature contraction with the tongue tip brought forward into contact with the LOWER lip; distractors are 'palatal to upper incisors' / 'posterior tongue activity'.

Flush/mesial step with favorable forward mand growth ends up Class I; DISTAL step ends up Class II — they swap the terminal plane each year, you must know each outcome.

Ch 4 Concepts of Growth & Development 5 HY · 15 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
First events of puberty — location
Puberty's first events occur in the BRAIN (hypothalamus/CNS triggers the cascade), not the gonads (Proffit p.84).
Neural growth — age complete
Neural tissue (brain/skull/CNS) growth is essentially COMPLETE by ~6-7 years of age (~90-95% of adult size); follows the neural curve, which plateaus early.
Cause of late lower-incisor crowding
LATE MANDIBULAR GROWTH (late differential anterior growth + decreasing arch length), NOT third molars, is the major cause of late mandibular incisor crowding.
Third molars and lower anterior crowding
Lower anterior crowding develops EVEN WITH congenitally missing 3rd molars; therefore 3rd molars do NOT cause the crowding and prophylactic 3rd-molar extraction to prevent/relieve crowding is NOT recommended.
Bjork's implant studies — internal (true/intramatrix) rotation
Internal/true rotation = rotation of the mandibular CORE relative to the cranial base, revealed only by metallic implants; averages ~15 degrees of forward rotation over the growth period.
Bjork — matrix rotation (Fig 4.14B)
Matrix rotation = rotation of the lower mandibular border (mandibular plane) relative to the cranial base; surface remodeling masks the larger internal rotation so the net change in mandibular plane angle is small.
Figure 4.3 identity
Fig 4.3 = VELOCITY curve of growth (growth RATE plotted against age); demonstrates the pubertal growth spurt and that girls reach it ~2 years before boys.
Figure 4.2 identity (Scammon curves)
Fig 4.2 = Scammon's growth curves; the overall/general (somatic) curve is S-shaped/sigmoid, separate from neural (early plateau), lymphoid (peaks then declines), and genital (late surge) curves.
Vertical eruption mechanism in growing child
Vertical position change of incisors during growth = continued TOOTH ERUPTION compensating for vertical facial growth (active eruption), NOT passive eruption (gingival migration exposing crown).
Passive vs active eruption
Active eruption = actual occlusal movement of the tooth (compensates for vertical jaw growth); passive eruption = apparent lengthening of clinical crown from apical gingival migration with no tooth movement.
Chipped/abraded incisor — which tissue increases
DENTIN increases (secondary/reparative dentin deposition continues throughout life); enamel cannot regenerate and pulp recedes with age.
Retained primary 1st & 2nd molars (no exfoliation) effect on arch length
Arch length STAYS THE SAME (is maintained) while primary molars are retained — premature loss is what shortens arch length via mesial drift; retention preserves leeway space.
Adult profile change — nose
The NOSE continues to grow (becomes larger/more prominent) into adulthood as part of continued soft-tissue change after skeletal growth slows.
Adult profile change — lips
Lips FLATTEN / become thinner and less prominent with age (relative retrusion) as the nose and chin continue forward growth.
Gingival smile in a 13-yr-old (growing patient) management
INTRUDE the incisors (correct vertical maxillary excess of the dentoalveolar component) rather than reassure/extrude — incisor display is reduced by intrusion.

Continued late/adult facial change: nose grows, lips flatten/thin; mandible rotates and translates down-and-forward — soft-tissue profile keeps changing after growth is 'complete'.

Ch 5 Etiology of Malocclusion & Craniofacial Syndromes 12 HY · 24 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
Craniofacial (hemifacial) microsomia — cause
Insult to neural crest cells during early development (not methanol/alcohol per se); a teratogenic insult to neural crest. Unilateral asymmetry, microtia, deficient ramus/condyle.
Treacher Collins syndrome — cause/gene
Genetic mutation in TCOF1 gene (autosomal dominant). NOT drug-induced. Bilateral and symmetric.
Pierre Robin & Treacher Collins — embryologic origin
Defect of the 1st pharyngeal (branchial) arch.
Craniofacial microsomia + Pierre Robin — pharyngeal arch
1st pharyngeal arch.
Pierre Robin sequence — triad
Micrognathia (small mandible at birth) + glossoptosis (tongue falls back) + airway obstruction; often with cleft palate (Fig 5-14).
Pierre Robin — 3yo with breathing difficulty, treatment
Mandibular distraction osteogenesis (to advance mandible/open airway).
Severe syndromic mandibular deficiency (Fig 5.11) — definitive treatment
Wait until growth is complete, then orthognathic surgery.
Treacher Collins — clinical features
Bilateral hypoplasia of zygoma/maxilla, down-slanting palpebral fissures, lower-eyelid coloboma, ear (pinna) deformities.
Fetal alcohol syndrome — discriminating feature
Midface deficiency / deficiency of midline tissue (also flat midface, thin/smooth upper lip, indistinct philtrum, micrognathia). NOT macrognathia.
Cleft lip — fusion failure (right-sided)
Failure of fusion between the (ipsilateral) medial nasal process and the maxillary process — right cleft = right medial nasal + maxillary process.
Cleft lip & palate — main risk factor
Maternal cigarette smoking (also a recognized teratogen/risk factor); NOT Zika or radiation in this bank.
Etiology of malocclusion / crowding
Multifactorial — caused by heredity AND environmental factors together (hereditary with environmental influence). Not purely genetic, not purely environmental.
Most common cause of facial asymmetry
Fracture of the condyle (condylar fracture).
Late-developing unilateral posterior open bite/crossbite after ortho (retainer stops fitting)
Condylar / hemimandibular hyperplasia (unilateral overgrowth).
PA cephalogram — mandibular shift with one ramus longer
Differential includes condylar hyperplasia (vs bifid condyle / condylar hypoplasia); asymmetric ramus length = condylar hyperplasia.
Adenoid (long) face — intraoral features
High (narrow/vaulted) palate and constricted (narrow) maxilla; long-face mouth-breathing pattern.
Effect of adenoidectomy on growth
Growth tends to improve / normalize (returns toward normal) after adenoid removal.
Thumb-sucking V-shaped arch constriction — mechanism
Caused by greater pressure at the CORNERS of the mouth (cheek pressure), not by negative intraoral pressure.
Anterior open bite — molar component
~8 mm open bite ≈ about 4 mm of posterior molar extrusion (over-eruption of molars contributes to the open bite).
Open bite treatment in children — complicating/associated factors
Posterior crossbite, over-eruption of upper molars, and persistent thumb-sucking habit complicate treatment.
Ectodermal dysplasia — diagnostic triad
Extensive hypodontia/oligodontia + sparse hair (hypotrichosis) + lack of sweating (hypohidrosis).
Cleidocranial dysplasia — distinguishing
Distinguish from ectodermal dysplasia: CCD has supernumerary/unerupted teeth + absent/hypoplastic clavicles (not the hair/sweat findings).
Rubella (congenital) infection — classic triad
Microphthalmia/cataracts, deafness (sensorineural), and cardiac/CNS defects — pediatric cataracts + deafness = congenital rubella virus.
Tongue thrust
Identified as the cause in the tongue-thrust image — anterior tongue posture/thrust associated with open bite.

Ch 6 Orthodontic Diagnosis: Esthetics, Asymmetry & Imaging 9 HY · 31 memorize

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
Golden proportion (anterior teeth)
62% — each tooth appears 62% the width of the tooth immediately mesial to it when viewed from the frontal view.
Ideal crown height-to-width ratio (maxillary central incisor)
~80% (width is about 80% of height); a tooth measuring 0.8mm length x 0.8mm width (1:1) looks unesthetic due to ABNORMAL SHAPE.
Bolton discrepancy — clinically significant threshold
A tooth-size discrepancy of 1.5 mm is considered clinically significant.
Ideal lip coverage of upper incisors (adult / 16 yr old)
1–2 mm of the upper incisor covered by the lip at rest is ideal.
Lip-to-tooth relationship at end of adolescence (finishing)
Finish with MORE upper-incisor coverage than the adult ideal — because incisors continue to erupt / face lengthens, so leaving more show now becomes ideal later.
Maximum lip separation at smile (all racial groups)
Maximum lip separation in a full smile is ~3 mm gingival display considered the upper limit of normal (Fig 6.17).
Acceptable SN–FH angle
6–7° is the normal/acceptable angle between the SN line and the Frankfort Horizontal plane.
Roll deformity (cant)
ROLL = transverse cant of the dentition/occlusal plane — one side higher/lower than the intercommissure line; best diagnosed clinically with a TONGUE DEPRESSOR.
Pitch deformity
PITCH = vertical (anteroposterior tip) discrepancy of gingival/incisal display — too much or too little tooth/gum shown.
Yaw deformity
YAW = horizontal rotation of the dental arch (midline / one side forward), producing a midline-type discrepancy.
Sassouni analysis
FIRST cephalometric analysis to emphasize VERTICAL as well as horizontal relationships; the analysis of choice for skeletal OPEN BITE / vertical problems.
Sassouni — planes meeting close to the face
When the planes intersect close to the face and diverge anteriorly (long anterior / short posterior face height) it predisposes to SKELETAL OPEN BITE; planes meeting far/behind = deep bite.
Lisp (sibilant speech distortion) — dental cause
Caused by ANTERIOR OPEN BITE with spaced incisors / a large gap between the incisors.
Sleep apnea — diagnostic test
POLYSOMNOGRAPHY is the diagnostic test for obstructive sleep apnea.
Corticosteroids — effect on tooth movement
Corticosteroids DELAY / slow orthodontic tooth movement.
Gold standard to assess completed growth (pre-orthognathic)
SERIAL superimposed cephalometric radiographs taken over time — growth is complete when no change is seen between tracings.
Best X-ray to localize an impacted/ectopic canine
CBCT (3D localization); periapical/occlusal (parallax) are alternatives but CBCT is best.
CBCT field of view to minimize radiation
Use a SMALL (limited) field of view to reduce radiation dose to only the region of interest.
CBCT — reduce radiation via voxel size
Use a LARGER voxel size (lower resolution) to reduce radiation dose.
PA (posteroanterior) cephalogram — purpose
Used to assess FACIAL ASYMMETRY (transverse/frontal-plane discrepancies).
Mesiodens vs impacted canine (most significant finding)
When both present, the MESIODENS is the most clinically significant diagnosis to address first.
Stereolithographic (3D-printed) model for tooth autotransplantation — advantage
REDUCES manipulation/handling of the donor tooth (preserves PDL vitality; less chair/extraoral time).
Headgear — immunization history relevance
Need to confirm immunization because of RISK OF INJURY from the headgear (facebow recoil/penetrating eye injury) — tetanus prophylaxis concern.
Flat / reverse smile arc with lip covering incisors — correction
EXTRUDE the UPPER incisors to create a consonant (curved) smile arc.
Gummy smile in an 11-year-old — management
REASSURE — excess gingival display is NORMAL at this age (incisors still erupting / passive eruption incomplete).
Best method to visualize/measure crowding
Study CASTS — they allow direct measurement of the amount of crowding (better than photographs).
Best clinical exam for a cant / canting
TONGUE DEPRESSOR held across the occlusal plane (compared to the interpupillary/intercommissure line).
Class III with permanent dentition + immature CVS (growing) — diagnosis
GROWING Class III (still has growth potential per immature cervical vertebral maturation), not adult or pseudo Class III.
Class III + posterior crossbite child with caries — treatment
Hyrax (rapid maxillary EXPANSION) then Delaire FACEMASK / protraction (after addressing caries).
Everted/protruded lower lip with short anterior face height — cause
OVERCLOSURE (decreased lower face height) everts the lip.
Cephalic-line identification (E/S/H/Z lines)
Recognize soft-tissue reference lines: E-line (Ricketts, nose tip to chin), S-line (Steiner), H-line (Holdaway, to upper lip), Z-line (Merrifield).

Bisphosphonates - effect on tooth movement
DECREASE bone remodeling -> SLOW / reduced / difficult tooth movement; impair osteoclast function; risk of medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ).
RANKL (gene therapy)
Transferring RANKL to the PDL STIMULATES osteoclastogenesis -> ACCELERATES tooth movement. High RANKL = fast movement.
OPG (osteoprotegerin)
Decoy receptor that BLOCKS RANKL -> INHIBITS osteoclasts -> SLOWS / stops tooth movement (used as anchorage gene, opposite of RANKL).
Runx2 / VEGF
Distractors in the 'fast movement gene' question - the correct accelerating gene is RANKL, not Runx2 or VEGF.
Most accepted theory of tooth movement
Pressure-tension theory (biochemical/cellular response in PDL); bone bending/bioelectric is the older alternative.
Light force for tipping vs bodily movement
Tipping concentrates force over a SMALL area of PDL (at the alveolar crest/apex), so lighter force is used than for bodily movement which spreads stress evenly over whole PDL.
Optimal orthodontic force type
Light continuous force -> frontal (direct) resorption; heavy force -> hyalinization + undermining resorption (delayed movement, more root resorption).
Frontal vs undermining resorption
Frontal (direct) = light forces, no PDL crushing, efficient. Undermining (indirect) = heavy force crushes PDL -> hyalinized zone -> resorption from adjacent marrow spaces -> delay + pain.
Most common early side effect (first weeks/months)
Pain / pulpal-PDL sensitivity for ~2-3 days after each adjustment (peaks ~24h, resolves in days) - this is the MOST common effect, not root resorption.
Root resorption - highest-risk movement
INTRUSION carries the highest root resorption risk (also torque and prolonged heavy/continuous force, bodily movement).
Root resorption - highest-risk teeth
Maxillary (UPPER) central incisors are most prone to orthodontic root resorption.

Cortical anchorage
Roots torqued against dense cortical bone resist movement more (slower resorption) - used to reinforce anchorage, e.g., in prognathic-mandible cases refusing surgery.
Reciprocal anchorage
Two teeth/units of roughly equal resistance move toward each other equally (e.g., closing a midline diastema); both units move.
Reinforced anchorage
Adding more teeth/units (or appliances like TPA, Nance, headgear) to the anchor unit to increase resistance.
First-order bend
In-out bend (buccolingual/labiolingual positioning) - includes toe-in/toe-out.
Second-order bend
Tip / mesiodistal angulation bend (e.g., tip-back, gable bends).

Torque - buccolingual root/crown inclination (labiolingual crown torque).

Continuous = NiTi coil/archwire (force maintained between visits); interrupted = force drops to zero then reactivated; intermittent = force reduced to zero when appliance removed (elastics, removable).

Controlled tipping (Mc/Mf ratio)
0 < Mc/Mf < 1 produces controlled tipping. The recurring exam value Mc=100, Mf=90 (ratio ~1.1) is treated as controlled tipping / approaching root movement.
Bodily movement (translation) ratio
Mc/Mf = 1 (≈10:1 moment-to-force, ~10 g·mm per g) produces bodily movement / translation.
Torque / root movement ratio
Mc/Mf > 1 produces root movement (torque); pure tipping = Mc/Mf = 0 (no counter-couple).
Centered (symmetric) V-bend
A V-bend at the MIDPOINT between two teeth produces equal and opposite (symmetric) couples/moments on both teeth, with no net vertical force.
Off-center V-bend (e.g. 1/3 distance, close to molar)
An asymmetric V-bend creates a larger moment on the CLOSER tooth; the closer tooth (molar) gets the dominant moment and the system produces intrusion/extrusion (off-center → unequal moments).
Step bend vs V-bend
A step bend produces equal and opposite moments regardless of position; a V-bend's moments depend on position along the wire.
Beam length vs range (cantilever power law)
Range varies with the SQUARE of length: doubling beam length → range × 4 (and springiness × 8, but strength halved). Range of A = 4 when B = 1 in the figure.
Beam diameter vs properties (power laws)
For a round cantilever: strength ∝ d³, stiffness ∝ d⁴, range (springiness) ∝ 1/d⁴ — doubling diameter increases stiffness 16×, strength 8×, reduces range to 1/16.
Stiffness vs springiness vs range relationship
Stiffness = slope of load-deflection curve = 1/springiness. Stiffness is directly proportional to modulus of elasticity (E); springiness is inversely related to stiffness.
Slope of load-deflection / stress-strain curve
The slope (E) of the elastic portion = stiffness/springiness of the wire (steepness of the curve = modulus of elasticity).
Yield point / proportional limit on stress-strain curve
The point where the curve deviates from linear = proportional limit / yield point; beyond it permanent deformation occurs. Ultimate tensile strength = highest point; failure point = end.
Lingual force for bodily movement (lever-arm balance)
Force is scaled by lever-arm ratio: buccal 60g at 15mm balanced by lingual at 20mm → lingual force = 60 × (15/20) = 45 g. For 100g buccal at 15mm vs 20mm lingual → 75 g.

Moment of couple = force × distance. 90g force, 12mm bracket-to-CR → 1080 g·mm total moment of couple.

Memorize answer = 200 g·mm moment on the anterior segment.
Titanium content vs friction
Friction/surface reactivity increases with titanium content; the highest-titanium option (80% titanium / β-Ti TMA) shows the greatest friction. (Pure Ti/TMA is the 'stickiest' wire.)
Friction ranking of archwire materials
Lowest to highest surface friction: stainless steel < cobalt-chromium < NiTi < β-titanium (TMA). SS slides best; TMA worst.
Resistance to sliding — binding dominates
Binding (and notching), not classical friction, is the major contributor to resistance to sliding once the wire-bracket angle exceeds the critical contact angle.
What affects binding
Bracket width (and wire-bracket angulation) affects binding; wider brackets / greater angulation increase binding.
Best sliding wire in an .018 slot
16 × 22 / 16 × 25 (a slightly undersized rectangular wire) is best for sliding in an .018 slot — gives control with minimal binding.
Corrosion type rarely seen in orthodontics
Stress corrosion is the type rarely seen clinically in orthodontics; the common one is pitting/crevice (and galvanic) corrosion.
Superelastic NiTi types (M-NiTi vs A-NiTi)
A-NiTi (austenitic, active) is the superelastic one showing the flat plateau; M-NiTi (martensitic, work-hardened) is non-superelastic. 'Active' = A-NiTi; M-NiTi inactive.
Superelastic plateau force delivery

Magnets — disadvantage
Force cannot be controlled — it changes unpredictably (inversely with the square of distance) as the tooth moves; force level is unpredictable (also bulky/toxicity concerns, but force control is the key answer).
Center of resistance with periodontal bone loss
Periodontal (bone) loss moves the center of resistance MORE APICAL (further down the root), increasing the moment for a given force.
Center of resistance location (normal tooth)
For a single-rooted tooth, CR is roughly 1/3 to 1/2 of the root length apical to the alveolar crest (about 24–35% of root length from crest).

★ MUST-MEMORIZE (rote — just know it)
Sulcular epithelium
Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium that HAS the potential to keratinize. (Junctional = non-keratinized, no potential; oral/masticatory = already keratinized.)
Gingival type differentiation
Determined by the underlying connective tissue / mucogingival junction, not the epithelium itself.
Branchial (lateral) cleft cyst
Swelling at/below angle of mandible, ANTERIOR to sternocleidomastoid, present since childhood, no nodal involvement, aspirate = yellowish-brown fluid; arises from 2nd branchial cleft remnant.
Ranula
Bluish swelling in the floor of mouth UNDER the tongue from sublingual/submandibular gland (mucus extravasation); mucocele = lip/minor gland.
Median rhomboid glossitis
Smooth, red, circumscribed depapillated area in the MIDLINE of dorsal tongue, anterior to circumvallate papilla; candidal etiology (formerly thought a lingual thyroid/developmental defect).
Warthin tumor
= Cystadenolymphoma (papillary cystadenoma lymphomatosum); benign tumor of the PAROTID gland; NOT a carcinoma.
Pleomorphic adenoma
Most common benign salivary (parotid) tumor; mixed tumor; slow painless expansion.
Adenoid cystic carcinoma
Salivary malignancy notorious for PERINEURAL invasion/spread (so causes pain/nerve signs).
Parotid tumor - significant sign
FACIAL NERVE PALSY is the most clinically significant finding (indicates malignancy); painless rapid enlargement also suggests malignancy.
Cherubism
5-year-old with BILATERAL multilocular expansile radiolucent mandibular lesions and displaced primary teeth = cherubism (autosomal dominant, SH3BP2 gene).
Lingual thyroid / median rhomboid lesion location
Midline dorsum of tongue (foramen cecum region), anterior to circumvallate papilla.
Pluripotent cells
Form all cell types of the body from all THREE germ layers (embryonic stem cells). Totipotent = can form whole organism incl. placenta; multipotent = limited lineages (adult/cord-blood stem cells).
Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)
Somatic cells genetically REPROGRAMMED back to a pluripotent state (can become any body cell type).
Gene to ACCELERATE orthodontic tooth movement
RANKL (transferred to PDL) — stimulates osteoclastogenesis/bone resorption. (OPG = decoy receptor, slows movement; Runx2 = osteoblast differentiation.)
Gene affecting mandibular/condylar growth
Runx2 (RUNX2) — controls condylar/mandibular growth and osteoblast differentiation.
Adenovirus (gene therapy vector)
Targets NON-dividing (quiescent) cells; does NOT integrate into host genome. Retrovirus requires actively DIVIDING cells and integrates.
Stem-cell transplant in animal models
Recipient mice must be IMMUNOCOMPROMISED / immunosuppressed (depress the immune system) to prevent rejection.
Corticosteroids on tooth movement
Decrease bone remodeling / increase risk of bone resorption; chronic use reduces controlled orthodontic movement (acute may transiently speed it).
Tooth structure with age (dentin)
DENTIN increases (secondary/tertiary dentin deposition); pulp chamber shrinks with age — so after a chip, dentin thickness increases.
Auricle (pinna) origin
Develops from the 1st AND 2nd pharyngeal (branchial) arches (six hillocks of His).
3rd pharyngeal arch skeletal derivative
Greater horn (greater cornu) and lower part of the body of the HYOID bone. (2nd arch = lesser horn + upper body.)
3rd pharyngeal POUCH
Derivatives = THYMUS + INFERIOR parathyroid glands. (4th pouch = SUPERIOR parathyroids + ultimobranchial body.)
Pharyngeal pouch vs cleft distinction
Thymus is from the pharyngeal POUCH (endoderm, 3rd), not the cleft. Pouches = endodermal (internal); clefts/grooves = ectodermal (external).
Facial nerve (CN VII) functions tested
Motor to muscles of facial expression — eyelid closure (orbicularis oculi); taste to anterior 2/3 tongue via chorda tympani.
Taste - anterior 2/3 vs posterior 1/3 of tongue
Anterior 2/3 taste = facial nerve (chorda tympani); posterior 1/3 taste = glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX).
Vagus nerve (CN X)
Elevates the soft palate ('say ah'); supplies most palatal & pharyngeal muscles (except tensor veli palatini = CN V3, and stylopharyngeus = CN IX).
Greater petrosal nerve
Branch of facial nerve carrying taste/secretomotor; a middle-cranial-fossa injury causing loss of PALATAL taste implicates the greater petrosal nerve.
Foramen ovale contents
Transmits mandibular nerve (CN V3); injury → loss of motor to muscles of mastication (e.g., anterior belly of digastric) and sensory of lower face — NOT forehead, NOT eye abduction.
Lateral pterygoid muscle
PROTRUDES the mandible and opens/translates; bilateral action causes protrusion; involved in open-lock dislocation where patient cannot close the mouth.
Cavernous sinus thrombosis route
Upper molar infection spreads to cavernous sinus via the PTERYGOID venous plexus (and ophthalmic veins) — valveless veins allow retrograde flow.
Subdural/base-of-skull hemorrhage source
Venous plexus / bridging veins implicated in base-of-brain (subdural) bleeding after head trauma.
Anterior disc displacement WITH reduction
Disc sits anterior to condyle at rest but RECAPTURES on opening, producing a reciprocal CLICK; jaw opening is normal range.
Anterior disc displacement WITHOUT reduction
Disc stays anterior throughout opening (no recapture), NO click, LIMITED opening ('closed lock').
TMJ crepitus
Grating sound from chronic disc displacement WITHOUT reduction or disc PERFORATION → bone-to-bone (condyle–fossa) contact.
Best imaging for TMJ disc perforation

Best imaging for TMJ soft tissue/disc position

Reverse Towne view

Radiosensitive cells
Most radiosensitive: small LYMPHOCYTES, bone marrow/hematopoietic cells, reproductive (germ) cells, immature bone/osteoblasts, intestinal crypt cells (high mitotic rate).
Radioresistant cells
Most radioresistant: NERVE cells, MUSCLE cells, MATURE bone (low/no mitotic activity).
Absorbed dose
Energy deposited per unit mass of tissue; SI unit = gray (Gy). 'The dose of x-ray' absorbed = absorbed dose.
Equivalent dose
Absorbed dose weighted by radiation type (W_R); SI unit = sievert (Sv); reflects biological effectiveness.
Effective dose
Equivalent dose weighted by tissue radiosensitivity (W_T); SI unit = sievert (Sv); measures overall BIOLOGICAL effect/risk.
Radiation biological effect - mode of action
By MODE OF ACTION = DIRECT or INDIRECT (indirect via water radiolysis/free radicals). NOT somatic/genetic (that's by cell affected), NOT stochastic/deterministic (that's by dose-response).
Stochastic vs deterministic effects
Stochastic = no threshold, probability rises with dose (cancer, genetic mutation); Deterministic = threshold dose, severity rises with dose (erythema, cataract).
Somatic vs genetic effects
Somatic = affect the irradiated individual; Genetic = affect offspring (germ-cell damage).
